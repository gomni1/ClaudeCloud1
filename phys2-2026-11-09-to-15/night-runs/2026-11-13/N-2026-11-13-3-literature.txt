# Literature location: positive weights and uncertainty floors

Compiled 2026-11-13. Web search + page fetch only. "Fetched" = I read the page via fetch; "located" = search result / DOI found but body not read; "from memory, not sourced" = background knowledge, not checked tonight.

## 1. Epistemic-restriction results

**Bartlett, Rudolph, Spekkens (2012)**, "Reconstruction of Gaussian quantum mechanics from Liouville mechanics with an epistemic restriction", Phys. Rev. A 86, 012103; arXiv:1111.5057. https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5057 (fetched via ar5iv, first ~100k chars only).
- What is restricted: the set of allowed Liouville (positive phase-space) distributions. Two postulated conditions: (i) a "classical analogue of Heisenberg's uncertainty principle" - the second moments of the distribution must obey the same constraint as quantum moments (quantum condition quoted in the paper as gamma + i hbar Sigma >= 0, versus the bare classical condition gamma >= 0); (ii) "the distribution should have maximal entropy for the given moments". Together these force valid states to be Gaussians with quantum-admissible covariance.
- Reproduced: Gaussian quantum mechanics, operationally exactly; "complementary measurements", "noncommuting measurements", "collapse of the wavefunction", "no-cloning", teleportation, key distribution, error correction, "monogamy of pure entanglement".
- Not reproduced: "Bell inequality violations" (the theory "provides a local hidden variable model for Gaussian quantum mechanics"), "violations of operational noncontextuality inequalities", exponential computational speed-up, and "Quantum interference phenomena and quantization of quantities such as angular momentum and energy".
- Key point for the question: unrestricted Liouville mechanics has only gamma >= 0 (any covariance, including zero, is allowed). The floor is put in by hand as a rule on which distributions an agent may hold.

**Spekkens (2016)**, "Quasi-quantization: classical statistical theories with an epistemic restriction", in Quantum Theory: Informational Foundations and Foils (Springer), doi:10.1007/978-94-017-7303-4_4; arXiv:1409.5041. https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5041 (fetched via ar5iv, first ~100k chars).
- Restriction ("classical complementarity"): "one can have joint knowledge of a set of quadrature variables if and only if they commute" (Poisson bracket).
- Reproduced: for continuous variables "the Gaussian subtheory"; for odd-prime d "reproduces precisely the stabilizer theory for qudits". For bits it "is not operationally equivalent to the stabilizer subtheory" (a "distortion").
- Not reproduced: Bell-inequality violation, contextuality ("epistricted theories, considered as ontological models, are by construction both local and noncontextual"), computational speed-up.
- Status of the restriction: the theory "postulates, as a fundamental principle, that there is a restriction"; "an epistemic restriction is, strictly speaking, an assumption that goes beyond classical physics".

**Spekkens toy model**: R. W. Spekkens, "Evidence for the epistemic view of quantum states: A toy theory", Phys. Rev. A 75, 032110 (2007), arXiv:quant-ph/0401052 - from memory, not sourced tonight. "Knowledge balance principle": at most half the information needed to fix the ontic state may be known.

## 2. Joint distributions versus incompatibility

**Fine's theorem.** A. Fine, "Hidden variables, joint probability, and the Bell inequalities", Phys. Rev. Lett. 48, 291 (1982), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.48.291 (located); companion J. Math. Phys. 23, 1306 (1982) (from memory, not sourced). Statement as given in the Stanford Encyclopedia, "Bell's Theorem", note 5 (fetched: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bell-theorem/notes.html): for the Bell/CHSH scenario the following are equivalent - a deterministic hidden-variables model; a factorizable stochastic model; "one joint distribution for all observables of the experiment"; well-defined compatible joint distributions for all pairs and triples of commuting and noncommuting observables; "the Bell inequalities hold". Scope caution: this is about a single joint distribution reproducing a *family of measured pair marginals*, not about a variance-product floor.

**Hudson's theorem.** R. L. Hudson, "When is the Wigner quasi-probability density non-negative?", Rep. Math. Phys. 6, 249-252 (1974) (title located at https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:1224823; volume/pages from memory). Statement as quoted in D. Gross, "Hudson's theorem for finite-dimensional quantum systems", J. Math. Phys. 47, 122107 (2006), arXiv:quant-ph/0602001 (abstract fetched): "the Wigner function of a pure state has no negative values if and only if the state is Gaussian". Gross: in odd dimension the only pure states with non-negative discrete Wigner function are stabilizer states. Multi-mode extension Soto & Claverie, J. Math. Phys. 24, 97 (1983) - from memory, not sourced.

**Wigner / Cohen (from memory, not sourced).** Wigner (1971, "Quantum-mechanical distribution functions revisited") showed no phase-space function that is bilinear in psi, non-negative for all states, and has the correct x and p marginals exists. Cohen & Zaparovanny (J. Math. Phys. 21, 794, 1980) showed non-negative joint distributions with the correct quantum marginals do exist if bilinearity is dropped (e.g. the product |psi(x)|^2 |phi(p)|^2). Consequence: mere existence of a positive joint distribution with given x and p marginals does not remove the Heisenberg floor on those marginals; the floor is a constraint on which marginal pairs occur (Fourier pairing), not on positivity as such.

## 3. Classical "uncertainty relations" for diffusion

**Fürth (1933)**, "Über einige Beziehungen zwischen klassischer Statistik und Quantenmechanik", Z. Phys. 81, 143-162. English translation and commentary: L. Peliti and P. Muratore-Ginanneschi, Eur. Phys. J. H 48, 4 (2023), arXiv:2006.03740. https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03740 (fetched).
- Relation: eq. (38) mean(x^2) mean(v^2) >= D^2, written as eq. (39) Delta x Delta v >= D: "the product of the uncertainties must be always larger than the diffusion coefficient D".
- The two quantities: Delta x = sqrt(mean x^2) under the density u(x,t); v is NOT the particle's velocity. Fürth: "by no means this role can be played by the microscopic velocity produced by molecular collisions". He takes the diffusion current Q = -D grad u and defines v = Q/u = -D d(ln u)/dx (eqs. 32-34).
- Ensemble or history: ensemble. Fürth: "the velocity current is for given u a pure function of the position." The translators' appendix A.4 gives a pathwise-looking definition, "the current velocity is defined as the conditional expectation of the time symmetric increment" - still a conditional average over the ensemble at x. So the second width is the spread of a density-gradient field (the score function), and the inequality is the Cauchy-Schwarz / Cramér-Rao inequality Var(x) * Fisher information >= 1 in disguise.
- Fürth notes the classical Gaussian saturating state is "self-sustaining" whereas in the quantum case "the minimum condition is only instantaneously satisfied". Appendix A.3 summarises Fényes: the two relations "cannot have the same meaning".

**Muratore-Ginanneschi and Peliti (2023)**, "Classical uncertainty relations and entropy production in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics", J. Stat. Mech. (2023) 083202 (journal from DOI 10.1088/1742-5468/ace3b3; volume/page from memory), arXiv:2302.08290. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08290 (abstract fetched). "Fürth's uncertainty relations are a property enjoyed by martingales under the measure of a diffusion process"; extended to piecewise-deterministic and general Markov processes; gives lower bounds on entropy-production fluctuations in terms of current velocities.

**Stochastic mechanics.**
- E. Nelson, "Derivation of the Schrödinger equation from Newtonian mechanics", Phys. Rev. 150, 1079 (1966) - from memory, not sourced. Introduces current velocity v and osmotic velocity u = D grad ln rho with D = hbar/2m.
- L. de la Peña-Auerbach and A. M. Cetto, Phys. Lett. A 39, 65 (1972) - from memory, not sourced; referred to in Golin's abstract as the earlier position-momentum result.
- S. Golin, "Uncertainty relations in stochastic mechanics", J. Math. Phys. 26, 2781-2783 (1985), doi:10.1063/1.526700 (abstract located via scilit): "full equivalence between the uncertainty relations in stochastic mechanics and conventional quantum mechanics is established".
- D. de Falco, S. De Martino, S. De Siena, "Momentum from sample paths in stochastic mechanics", Lett. Nuovo Cimento 36, 457-460 (1983), doi:10.1007/BF02817983 (abstract fetched; body paywalled). Their PRL 49, 181 (1982) "Position-momentum uncertainty relations in stochastic mechanics" - from memory, not sourced.
- D. A. Trifonov, B. A. Nikolov, I. M. Mladenov, "On the Uncertainty Relations in Stochastic Mechanics", arXiv:0902.3880 (fetched, first part). They state sigma_x^2 sigma_pN^2 >= hbar^2/4 for the osmotic ("extra") momentum and that it "directly stems from well known Cramer-Rao inequality". I.e. the osmotic part of the floor is pure classical statistics of a density once D = hbar/2m is fixed.
- M. J. W. Hall and M. Reginatto, "Schrödinger equation from an exact uncertainty principle", J. Phys. A 35, 3289 (2002), arXiv:quant-ph/0102069; and "Quantum mechanics from a Heisenberg-type equality", arXiv:quant-ph/0201084 (located only). From memory: momentum fluctuation strength is postulated to be fixed by the position density; Fisher-information term then yields the quantum potential.

**Related (located/fetched, lower relevance).** A. R. Usha Devi and H. S. Karthik, "Uncertainty relations in the realm of classical dynamics", arXiv:1108.2682 (Am. J. Phys. 80, 708 (2012) from memory): for fixed-energy (microcanonical) classical ensembles the scaled variance product takes fixed nonzero values (oscillator 1/4, well 1/3). This is a restricted-ensemble statement, same logical type as item 1. "Uncertainty and Wigner negativity in Hilbert-space classical mechanics", arXiv:2602.10341 - located, not read.

## 4. Thermodynamic and first-passage uncertainty relations

- A. C. Barato and U. Seifert, "Thermodynamic uncertainty relation for biomolecular processes", Phys. Rev. Lett. 114, 158101 (2015), arXiv:1502.05944 (abstract fetched). Cost >= 2 k_B T / epsilon^2, epsilon = relative uncertainty of an integrated current (steps of a motor, molecules produced). Precision versus dissipation.
- T. R. Gingrich and J. M. Horowitz, "Fundamental bounds on first passage time fluctuations for currents", Phys. Rev. Lett. 119, 170601 (2017) (journal ref from memory), arXiv:1706.09027 (fetched). Eq. (2): Var(T)/<T>^2 >= 2/(<T> sigma), sigma = mean entropy-production rate. Timing precision versus dissipation; no spatial width.
- J. P. Garrahan, "Simple bounds on fluctuations and uncertainty relations for first-passage times of counting observables", Phys. Rev. E 95, 032134 (2017) (journal ref from memory), arXiv:1701.00539 (fetched). Eq. (39): var(tau)/A >= 1/(<a><k>); FPT precision bounded by dynamical activity <k>. No spatial width.
- Answer: none of the three bounds a product of a timing spread and a spatial spread. Each bounds one relative spread from below by an inverse cost (entropy produced, or number of jumps). The cost can be made as large as one likes within the model class, so they are trade-offs with a resource, not a fixed floor on two widths. From memory, not sourced: applying the TUR to displacement in biased diffusion gives <x>^2/Var(x) <= sigma t / 2, again one variable against dissipation. I did not find any published TUR-type bound of the form Delta t * Delta x >= const.

## 5. Telegraph process / checkerboard

- B. Gaveau, T. Jacobson, M. Kac, L. S. Schulman, "Relativistic extension of the analogy between quantum mechanics and Brownian motion", Phys. Rev. Lett. 53, 419-422 (1984), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.53.419. Original not fetched (paywalled); confirmed through two secondary sources:
  - N. Barankai, "On the positivity preservation of the free Euclidean Dirac equation", arXiv:1612.05055 (fetched): "the analytic continuation of the parameter lambda to pure imaginary values by the replacement lambda -> mc^2/(i hbar) leads to the equation of motion of a massive Dirac particle"; the real-lambda equation for p_+/- "preserves the non-negativity and normalization"; Feynman's weights are "complex amplitudes" proportional to i dt whereas the stochastic version uses "the probability lambda dt for the reversals".
  - P. Ghose, "Electrodynamics as a Theory of Persistent Stochastic Processes", arXiv:2605.24494 (fetched): the Kac process "becomes directly related to the Dirac equation after analytic continuation of the reversal rate".
  - Also from memory, not sourced: M. Kac, Rocky Mountain J. Math. 4, 497 (1974); T. Jacobson and L. S. Schulman, J. Phys. A 17, 375 (1984).
- A plain statement that the real-rate telegraph process has no position-momentum uncertainty relation: NOT FOUND in anything I could fetch. Neither Barankai nor Ghose discusses uncertainty or interference.
- On what the continuation adds: G. N. Ord, "The Dirac Equation in Classical Statistical Mechanics", arXiv:quant-ph/0206016 (fetched) says "the FAC [formal analytic continuation] destroys any coherent interpretation of our microscopic model", and instead gets Dirac from a real signed ("net charge") density over paths - i.e. the weights are real but not positive; they "are not quantum mechanical amplitudes". This supports "positivity is what must go", but Ord does not discuss uncertainty.

## 6. Dispersion-free states / conditioning

- J. S. Bell, "On the problem of hidden variables in quantum mechanics", Rev. Mod. Phys. 38, 447 (1966), doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.38.447 (fetched from an MIT-hosted PDF). Hidden-variable states are such that "given values of these variables together with the state vector determine precisely the results of individual measurements"; these "hypothetical well-specified states are said to be 'dispersion free.'" Quantum states would be, "as in classical statistical mechanics", "averages over" better-defined states. Von Neumann excluded dispersion-free states by assuming additivity of expectation values for non-commuting observables; Bell: "There is no reason to demand it individually of the hypothetical dispersion free states."
- J. von Neumann, Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik (1932), ch. IV - from memory, not sourced: a statistical ensemble that admits dispersion-free sub-ensembles is exactly what he sought to exclude; in classical statistical mechanics every ensemble decomposes into dispersion-free (delta-function) ones.
- The explicit sentence "for any classical stochastic process one can condition on sub-ensembles arbitrarily sharp in two variables at once, because a joint distribution exists": NOT FOUND verbatim. The nearest published forms are (a) Bell/von Neumann's dispersion-free-state language above, and (b) Bartlett-Rudolph-Spekkens' contrast between gamma >= 0 (Liouville) and gamma + i hbar Sigma >= 0 (quantum), with the latter needing a postulate.

## What the literature already settles, and what it does not

Settled: (1) A positive-weight theory over a state space where both variables have definite values per history has no intrinsic floor on the product of their spreads: its covariance matrix need only be positive semidefinite, and it decomposes into dispersion-free states. A Heisenberg-type floor appears only if the allowed distributions are restricted by postulate (Bartlett-Rudolph-Spekkens; Spekkens: "an assumption that goes beyond classical physics"), and even then only the Gaussian/stabilizer fragment is recovered - no interference, contextuality or Bell violation. (2) Positive-weight models do have genuine, unconditional floors of the Fürth type, Delta x * Delta v >= D, but the second quantity is the osmotic velocity -D d(ln u)/dx, a functional of the ensemble density rather than a property of an individual history; the bound is the Cramér-Rao/Fisher inequality, and the stochastic-mechanics literature (Trifonov et al., Golin, de la Peña-Cetto) recovers the quantum relation through exactly this term with D = hbar/2m. Re-conditioning the ensemble changes the density and therefore changes that "velocity", so this floor does not survive as a statement about two history-level variables. (3) Thermodynamic and first-passage uncertainty relations bound a single relative spread against dissipation or activity, not a product of two widths. (4) The real-rate telegraph process obeys a positivity-preserving probability equation; Dirac arises only after continuing the rate to imaginary values (or, in Ord's variant, using signed real weights).

Not settled / not found: no located paper states as a theorem that "a positive-weight history rule cannot give a floor on (event-time spread) x (position spread)", and none treats an event-time/position pair at all; no located paper states plainly that the real-rate telegraph process lacks a position-momentum uncertainty relation; the post-selection argument appears only implicitly, as dispersion-free-state reasoning. Also open in the literature as located: whether a restriction of the Bartlett-Rudolph-Spekkens kind can arise dynamically (from the process itself limiting which ensembles can be prepared) rather than by postulate - the papers fetched treat it as a postulate. Caveats on this search: the GJKS PRL, Fine's PRL, Hudson's paper, Nelson, de la Peña-Cetto, and Golin were not read in full (paywalled or not fetched); ar5iv fetches of the two Spekkens papers covered only the first ~100k characters.
